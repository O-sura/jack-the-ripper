I stopped trusting password managers

18 August 2023

Unpopular opinion: handing every password I own to one company is a single point of failure, and I would rather that point of failure be me.

So I wrote my own. About forty lines of Python. Two years now with no subscription, no browser extension, and no company having a bad quarter at my expense.

Someone on DevForum asked to see it, so here it is. Be gentle.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""passgen.py - because I don't trust the cloud with my passwords."""

import random
import sys

ALPHABET = "abcdefghijkmnopqrstuvwxyzABCDEFGHJKLMNPQRSTUVWXYZ23456789"


def make(service, pin, length=14):
    random.seed(service.strip().lower() + str(pin))
    return "".join(random.choice(ALPHABET) for _ in range(length))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    service = sys.argv[1] if len(sys.argv) > 1 else input("service: ")
    pin     = sys.argv[2] if len(sys.argv) > 2 else input("pin: ")
    print(make(service, pin))

Download: passgen.py

One piece of advice if you are going to use it: pick a PIN you genuinely could not forget if you tried. Not a random number — the second you have to write it down somewhere you are storing passwords again, and the entire point is gone. Your birthday, something along those lines. Mine has not let me down in two years.

Yes, I know what some of you are going to say. It works for me.
